## Observation: shared SIX10 pseudogenization across three genomes, including the reference strain

During translation-based classification, SIX10 was independently classified as
pseudogenized (frame-selected translation carrying premature internal stop
codons) in three genomes: GCA_003315725.1 (isolate 4287 / Fol4287, f. sp.
lycopersici, Spain), GCA_003977725.1 (race 3, f. sp. lycopersici, USA), and
GCA_013423245.1 (f. sp. lini, Russia).

Direct comparison of the three translated sequences showed:
- GCA_003315725.1 and GCA_003977725.1 (both f. sp. lycopersici): translated
  protein sequences are byte-identical, including the positions of both
  premature stop codons.
- GCA_013423245.1 (f. sp. lini, a different forma specialis): the same two
  stop-codon positions are conserved, but the surrounding amino acid sequence
  shows several substitutions relative to the other two.

This pattern -- identical stop-codon placement conserved across a
forma-specialis boundary, with sequence divergence elsewhere -- is more
consistent with a single ancestral pseudogenization event that predates the
divergence of these lineages (deep vertical inheritance of a loss-of-function
allele) than with three independent pseudogenization events, since independent
premature stops landing at the same codon positions by chance is a much less
parsimonious explanation.

Note of particular relevance: GCA_003315725.1 is the Fol4287 reference strain,
required as the synteny anchor in the SIX7-SIX10-SIX12 analysis. Its SIX10
locus should be reported as pseudogenized (not simply absent or present) when
that analysis is written up.

This observation cannot be fully resolved until the core-genome phylogeny
(Part 9) is available. If Fol4287 and the race 3 isolate cluster closely
together with the f. sp. lini isolate placed considerably more distantly, that
topology would support the shared-ancestral-loss interpretation. Genuine
convergent pseudogenization at identical codon positions across a deep split
would be the less likely alternative and should only be favored if the tree
argues against shared ancestry at this depth.

To be carried into the Part 9 integration table as supporting evidence for the
"vertical inheritance" and/or "incomplete assembly" explanations (the latter
ruled less likely here, since the pattern is a consistent internal stop, not
a coordinate at a contig edge or an assembly-boundary flag).
